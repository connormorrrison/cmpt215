ANSWER: 
The original recurrence formula exhibits what is called "catastrophic cancellation." This phenomenon occurs when subtracting two closely approximated numbers results in a highly inaccurate estimate of their difference.

1. When t_i is small, t_i^2 is even smaller.
2. This makes sqrt(t_i^2 + 1) very close to 1.
3. When we subtract 1 from a number very close to 1, we lose many significant digits.

Since we only have a finite level of precision, these very small differences cannot be accurately represented. So, as the recurrence function accumulates values to compute the final value, the precision loss is accumulated, leading to a significant discrepancy in the final value.

The improved formula in (c) eliminates this subtraction, preserving more precision and produces better results.
